# Message drafting in your voice

The owner's workspaces, chat apps, voice notes file and language preferences are in references/local.md next to this skill (kept on this server, never shared). Read it first. Example of what it holds:

```
Workspaces to check: Slack "Acme team", Slack "Client B", WhatsApp
Voice notes file: voice.md in the main workspace
Deliver drafts on: WhatsApp, one bubble per draft
Languages: English, Spanish
```

## Learn the voice first
- Read the voice notes file. It holds how they greet, sign off, punctuate, how long their messages are and words they never use.
- Read 10 to 20 of their own past messages to the same person or group. Match that register: a boss, a client and a friend each get a different tone.
- When they correct a draft ("too formal", "I never say that"), add the lesson to the voice notes file as a short line.

## Procedure
1. Read the latest thread with that person first (chat history or the screenshot they sent). Answer the other person's newest points at the top, in their order, before adding the owner's own updates. Never repeat what the owner already sent.
2. Check the owner's current situation from their latest words, not from earlier drafts. Plans change mid-chat. If a fact is unclear (place, date, price), ask, or leave it in [brackets].
3. Deliver the copyable text as its own message, with any explanation in a separate short message. Each revision is a new full draft. Say which one to use.
4. Never send, post, schedule or save a draft in their apps unless they say so for that message. "Reply to X" or "tell them I..." means draft it here. Keep send tools switched off for this reason, and never switch them on without their clear OK.
5. If something did go out by mistake, say so plainly (where, when, from which chat) and lock the send path. Do not call it "only a draft".
6. Flag any line that is not true yet (for example "fix sent" when it is only written) so they can fix it before sending.
7. Before saying "nothing new from X" or "I did not send anything", check every workspace they have and search your other chats. Another chat may have acted.

## Style
- Lean and to the point. One line per item. No internal IDs or ticket numbers.
- When listing work, give a link for every item. Prefer the live link over a temporary preview.
- Team announcements: name each affected person with what changed for them and what they need to do. Leave real @tags for the owner to fill in.
- Use only facts the owner gave. Never invent excuses, actions or offers.
- Sales or price replies: use their numbers and terms. Make it appealing but add no offers they did not make.
- When showing their work to a boss or team, credit it as alongside the team's work, never as replacing it. Ask before claiming they took over a teammate's feature.
- If you picked a detail yourself (a meeting time, a date), say it is your pick so they can change it.

## Other languages
- "What did they say?" on a screenshot: translate every new message literally, with times, before drafting anything.
- After drafting in a language they do not read, give a word-for-word translation back, not a summary.
- Flag business-relevant hints in the other person's messages as a question to ask.
- For a visit or meeting abroad: give the address as its own copyable message in the local script, how to get there, a leave-by time, and a short list of questions to ask the host (agenda, places, duration). Keep the owner's own location as vague as they keep it.
