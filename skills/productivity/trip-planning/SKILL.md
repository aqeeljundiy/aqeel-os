# Trip planning

The person plans a trip step by step and sends many short follow-ups, often with phone screenshots of travel apps. You research, plan and save options. You never book, pay, or type card or one-time code data: find options, save or favourite them, then the person pays.

The person's own preferences (language, food needs, budget, home currency, favourite booking site, passport country) are in references/local.md next to this skill (kept on this server, never shared). Example:

```
- Reply language: English, short
- Passport: Acme Republic; home currency: ACM
- Food: vegetarian; must-have in hotel: quiet room
- Booking site used in the browser: travel.example
```

## Always-on rules
- When the person only asks about a place, answer it and leave the plan as it is. Change the schedule only when they say so.
- When they ask for a recap or notes, give one clean version: day by day schedule with the cost of each place, then hotels, things to book and when, apps, internet, food, budget. Drop the explanations.
- Respect food and personal needs in every tip (for example halal, vegetarian, allergies). Say plainly when an area is a poor fit.
- Take today's date from the session before writing "today" or "tomorrow".
- Offer a reminder for timed ticket releases (tickets that open N days ahead), set about 15 minutes before release, and confirm the exact time back.

## Procedure
1. **Basics.** Confirm dates, departure city, number of travellers, and passport. Flag tight timelines.
2. **Entry rules.** Check visa and entry rules for their passport on official sources (the destination's embassy or immigration site), because rules change. Also check online arrival forms, transit rules and passport validity. Say the source.
3. **Route.** One direction, no backtracking. Day trips from one hotel beat extra hotel changes. Check closure days and put busy attractions on weekdays.
4. **Flights.** Before they buy, compare fares into and out of each candidate gateway city (open-jaw is fine) and tell them the gap, even when they named an order. When reshuffling, price every leg of each order and show the totals side by side; say when the gap is small so the plan is not changed for nothing. With separate tickets, leave 5 to 6 hours or more before an international departure. Point out baggage limits and change fees.
5. **Hotels.** Search their exact dates. Prefer high guest rating, near public transport, free cancellation, and accepts foreign guests. Quote the real total for the stay, not "from" prices. Give tiers when asked (for example standard and cheap): name, rating, price per night, total, one-line why, caveats. End with a total range per tier.
6. **Judge a hotel against the plan,** not just the card: district and distance to each day's spots (km and transit time), rating and review count, price for the full stay and room type, cancellation, food nearby. For a question like "is the bathroom clean?", read the review sub-scores and review text, and say plainly when no review mentions it.
7. **Payments abroad.** Check which payment apps and cards work at the destination, how to verify an account as a visitor, and give the fallback (cash in small notes, prepaid bookings). Never suggest using another person's identity or card to pass verification.
8. **Daily plan.** Time blocks per day with a named food stop for each meal where it matters, ordered by transit line or geography, with the sunset or lights stop last. Mark times as estimates and say to re-check opening hours in a map app. Name which stop to drop first if the day is too long.
9. **Apps and setup.** Say exactly which button to press. Watch for wrong look-alike apps on screenshots.
10. **Booking via the browser.** Search and save favourites on your own screen. Never press pay or enter card data. Verify each saved item yourself instead of trusting a counter, and tell the person to confirm in their own app.

## Pitfalls
- **Screenshots win over notes.** Flight, hotel and route facts come from the person's own screenshots. When one contradicts an earlier plan, say so and use the screenshot from then on. Do not read a countdown banner as a deadline unless the app says so.
- **"From" prices.** Search snippet prices are the cheapest date or one night. Re-check for their dates before calling something cheap. A one-night price is not the nightly price for a longer stay.
- **Wrong hotel page.** In-site search often opens a different hotel. Compare the page title with the hotel asked about before quoting any score.
- **Search by landmark does not filter by area** on many booking sites. Use the site's area or station filter, or a landmark suggestion row, and say which you used.
- **Transit from memory is unreliable.** Take line names, directions and stop counts from the person's map app screenshot or a current source. Count back from the last connecting train, not the first line's last train.
- **Same-name branches.** A map search for a restaurant returns branches in other districts. Match the street address before quoting a distance.
- **One social post is not a fact.** Cross-check a second source for food needs and retract fast if fuller sources disagree.
- **Forms.** Decide per field: required or optional, and what it wants (birth city is not destination). Never invent values; ask or leave blank.
- **Card holds.** A large pending debit with a matching credit is a released authorization hold, not a charge.
- **Exchange rates.** Take the rate from the person's calculator screenshot or a fresh source, never from memory.
- **Unreadable attachments.** If an image or video did not load, say so and ask again. Never guess the content.
- **On the trip.** Answer as an in-the-moment helper: one clear next action first, the local-language text to show people, then a fallback.
- **Ticket products.** When an attraction page lists near-identical products, name the exact one to tap and what the others are. Add-ons like fast passes often exclude admission and are non-refundable; check the entry ticket exists before the day.
- **Health worries.** Give a non-diagnostic answer: likely causes, what to do now, red flags, and say you are not a doctor.
- **Late at night.** After answering, suggest they rest and continue in the morning instead of starting long research.
